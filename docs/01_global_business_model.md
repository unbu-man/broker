# Chapter I — Global Brokerage Business Model

## Outcome
Produce a defendable customer problem, value proposition, business-model comparison, and transparent unit-economic assumptions.

## Operating layers
1. **Fintech software company:** paid research tools, paper trading, risk education and enterprise software subject to applicable local law.
2. **Partner-integrated offering:** licensed partners undertake restricted activities. Partner functions, liability, permissions, geographic marketing and outsourcing must be contractually mapped.
3. **Own licensed brokerage:** the company itself becomes an authorized intermediary only after qualifying for all relevant licenses, capital, safeguarding and operational requirements.

## Revenue options
- Explicit subscriptions for educational software, research tools, and backtesting.
- Licensed B2B software and integration fees.
- Disclosed spreads, commissions or other charges **only** where appropriately authorized.
- A-Book: orders/exposure routed or hedged with external counterparties; net economics depend on costs.
- B-Book: the firm retains exposure and may gain when clients lose **or lose when clients win**; material conflicts, liquidity risks and regulatory constraints.
- Hybrid: mix of permitted hedging/internalization under independently verified risk and execution rules. This is not a license category.

## Never build revenue on misconduct
No manipulated prices, asymmetric slippage, suppressed withdrawals, hidden spreads, misleading odds, or promised returns. Monitor customer outcomes and trade quality, require best execution where applicable, disclose conflicts, and restrict products for unsuitable customers under relevant local rules.

## Decide first
- Founder-selected initial scope: **crypto + forex**, no stocks/ETFs at MVP. For engineering, start with simulated **spot crypto** and **FX pricing/order flows**, not live margined CFDs, leveraged derivatives or exchange custody.
- Decide later which regulated forex product and (if desired) crypto derivatives are commercially appropriate and lawful.
- Customer: retail education, professional research, or B2B broker technology.
- Market-entry order: not 'all countries' but one initial authorization/partner corridor followed by review of further countries.
- Client acquisition: lawful marketing channels, realistic acquisition cost, support language and banking/payment constraints.

## Unit economics worksheet
Contribution = subscriptions + allowed commissions/spreads + B2B fees + disclosed/allowed trading revenue
- liquidity/market risk P&L cost
- technology/data fees
- fraud/chargebacks
- customer support and KYC
- local compliance and banking cost.

Stress test negative trading results, sudden volatility, partner termination and low customer activity. Do not use user losses as guaranteed income.

## Definition of done
A one-page pitch for crypto/forex customers, three customer personas, comparative business-model table, 10 interview prompts, draft first country/client-market decision and honest risk statement.
